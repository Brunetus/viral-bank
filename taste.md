## universal
- Self-implication only works as a REVERSAL, not as a mood. "He was fine. I was
  the one who needed the chair" holds at 19.4% skip; the same speaker admitting
  the same thing without a turn ("he decides how it goes now, I just carry it")
  skips at 67.2%. The admission is not the mechanism. The flip from the expected
  subject to the actual one is.
- Hold and forwarding are usually different products — but the one post that did
  both did it with a verifiable artifact on screen. "The question on that screen
  is real" took 8.4s watch time AND 12 shares. Evidence the viewer can check
  travels; a claim about yourself only holds.
- A live countdown is the forwarding engine, and a stake beats a label. Three
  short "let's see how long this lasts" posts hold 61% of one feed's lifetime
  shares, while the same in-joke with no clock is a bottom-three hold. The format
  is not the asset, the unresolved outcome is.
- Do not re-run a hook inside 24 hours. The same caption posted twice in a day
  lost 67% of its views and its skip rate went 31.5% → 60.7%. A hook is spent
  once the audience has met it; the second run is not a second chance.
- Open on something the viewer has already met. A universally recognized object
  in frame one produces every low skip rate on record and consistently near-zero
  shares. Recognition buys attention, not reach.
- Never make the product itself the subject. Milestone and how-to-use-it posts
  hold the three worst skip rates on record (86.3%, 75.5%, 72.9%), across two
  unrelated accounts.
