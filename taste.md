## universal
- **A question the viewer wants to answer out-loud beats a statement.** The six widest
  reels this window are all a stranger's premise with one blank in it. The reel that
  merely *describes* the same idea sits 100x lower. Leave the blank; do not close it.
- **The blank must be filled by ONE ownable, picturable OBJECT.** Sharpest line in the
  data, five days running. Blanks answered by a single object ran 39k-1.31M views at
  1.4-4.1% shares. Blanks answered by a LIST, a REASON or an abstraction topped out at
  4,961 views and 0.27-0.80%. Same form, same craft, 100x apart.
- **The frame is reusable; only the OBJECT gets spent.** This is now measured, not
  inferred: the window's #1 premise was re-run with a DIFFERENT object and went
  120,679 → **1,311,822 views in 24 hours**, passing the original. A premise is not
  spent when it ships. Keep the question, swap the answer.
- **Shares, not likes, are the ceiling metric.** The settled top reel carries 32,300
  shares against 28,787 likes: more than one share per like, 19 days out. A cut
  engineered to be *sent to someone* outperforms a cut engineered to be admired.
- **Read share RATE late, and never rank on it.** Rate falls as reach widens: the biggest
  reel on the account reads 1.39% because its denominator exploded, while a reel 23x
  smaller reads 3.10%. Shares are the signal; shares ÷ views is a maturity marker, not a
  score. Give any reel 48h before reading either.
- **A low skip rate is a RETENTION signal, not a reach signal — never optimise for it.**
  The lowest-skip reel this window (28.2%) finished at 4,203 views; the wide ones sit at
  32-44%. Holding the people who stayed and being handed to new people are different machines.
- **Watch time is the same finding from the other side.** The longest-held reel (15.16s)
  did 3,020 views; the widest did 1.31M at 8.2s, and a 57k-view reel is the third
  SHORTEST hold on the account (5.7s). Length holds an audience you already have.
  A premise with a blank is what buys a new one.
- **Payoff last, and only once.** Every high-share reel lands its object in the final
  beat. Every low one answers itself in the first third, then keeps talking.
