## universal
- **Share rate is the distribution lever, not watch time.** The two widest reels this window
  held 7.8s and 8.2s average watch; the longest hold in the whole set (14.9s) reached 3,182
  people. A reel that is forwarded travels; a reel that is merely finished does not.
- **A premise with one blank in it travels; a statement does not.** Every reel over 100k this
  window opens on a claim plus its eliminations ("not money. not power.") and withholds the
  object. Every reel under 5k answers itself in the first sentence.
- **The payoff must be a thing the viewer owns, not a behaviour.** Named, picturable objects
  carry; adjectives and habits do not. Object COUNT is irrelevant — a one-object payoff
  (2.48% shares) and a three-object payoff (2.87%) land in the same band.
- **A frame is reusable; only the object is spent.** The same premise re-run with a fresh
  object did 823k and then 1.65M, five weeks apart. Rebuild the winner, swap the noun.
- **Open on the problem, never on the product.** The lowest-reach reels in both accounts open
  on a thing to go get; the highest open on a situation the viewer is already in.
- **Skip rate reads the first second, and it is the cheapest number to act on.** Under 25% the
  reel travels, over 60% nothing downstream can save it.

What works
- A positive superlative or desire about men, stated as a claim plus eliminations, blank withheld
  until the cut. Five of the account's six widest reels are this exact grammar.
- The payoff object is a thing the viewer owns and can picture: a 20x Max plan, tokens resetting,
  a published .com, a MacBook. Hardware and plans, never adjectives.
- Second-person address in the first word ("men,", "i have a question for the men") converts a
  statement into a question the tribe answers in the comments.
- Re-running a proven frame with a new object. Cheapest reach in the account's history.
What dies
- A premise the reel answers itself in the opening sentence (no blank left to fill).
- A payoff that is a behaviour, a setting or a workflow rather than an object.
- Self-narration — what he sold, what he tried, what he checked. No slot in it.
- A premise aimed at women about a man ("why he's not texting you", 1,216 views).

What works
- Opening on the kid or the chair as a situation the parent is already inside ("He decides how
  lunch goes now", "The highchair was never the problem"). Skip 19-25%, the account's best.
- The 14-month-old doing one specific thing. Concrete beats explanatory every time.
- A question the caption answers later ("will there be a highchair?") rather than a feature list.

- Explaining the idea instead of showing the meal. "That is the whole idea behind bambi" is the
  single worst-performing first frame in 23 reels.
