## universal
- The lowest skip rate on record now belongs to a first-person admission that
  the SPEAKER was the problem, and it happened on two unrelated feeds on the
  same day (18.6%, 18.1%). Both produced zero shares. Self-implication is the
  strongest hold mechanism measured, and it is not a reach mechanism.
- Hold and forwarding are different products. The top of the share table has
  never once been the top of the watch-time table. Decide which one a cut is
  for before writing the hook.
- A live countdown is the forwarding engine, and a stake beats a label:
  "let's see how long this lasts" holds 66% of one feed's lifetime shares,
  while the same in-joke without a clock is a bottom-three hold. The format is
  not the asset, the unresolved outcome is.
- Do not re-run a hook inside 24 hours. The same caption posted twice in a day
  lost 67% of its views and its skip rate went 31% → 61%. A hook is spent once
  the audience has met it; the second run is not a second chance.
- Open on something the viewer has already met. A universally recognized object
  in frame one produces every low skip rate on record and consistently zero
  shares. Recognition buys attention, not reach.
- Never make the product itself the subject. Milestone and how-to-use-it posts
  hold the two worst skip rates on record (75%, 86%), on two unrelated accounts.
