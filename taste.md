## universal
- Reach and hold are separate engines and a post can win one while losing the
  other. The longest hold ever recorded on either account (15.1s) reached 27
  people; the biggest reach ever recorded (5.9k) holds 7.0s. Optimise the hook
  for reach and the body for hold, and never read one as evidence of the other.
- The biggest post on record is an identity CONFESSION with a reversal in it, and
  it forwarded. "My whole career was being the person who says no to other
  people's spending" holds 5.9k views and 25 shares. Naming the old self you left
  is a reach move, not just a hold move.
- Self-implication only works as a REVERSAL, not as a mood. "He was fine. I was
  the one who needed the chair" holds at 19.2% skip; the same speaker admitting
  the same thing without a turn ("he decides how it goes now, I just carry it")
  skips at 67.6%. The admission is not the mechanism. The flip from the expected
  subject to the actual one is.
- Evidence the viewer can check travels; a claim about yourself only holds.
  Showing an actual screen and saying the thing on it is real took 8.3s watch
  time AND 12 shares. A verifiable artifact is the cheapest share trigger there is.
- A live countdown is a forwarding engine, and a stake beats a label. Short
  "let's see how long this lasts" posts with a visible clock hold 50% of one
  feed's lifetime shares, while the same in-joke with no clock is a bottom-three
  hold. The format is not the asset, the unresolved outcome is.
- Do not re-run a hook inside 24 hours. The same caption posted twice in a day
  lost 67% of its views and its skip rate went 31.6% → 60.7%. A hook is spent
  once the audience has met it; the second run is not a second chance.
- Never make the product itself the subject. Milestone and how-to-use-it posts
  hold the three worst skip rates on record (86.3%, 75.5%, 72.9%), across two
  unrelated accounts.
