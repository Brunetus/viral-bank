## universal
- Identity-confession hooks beat announcements. "I was a CFO and I left" /
  "I can't read a line of it" outruns "here is my title" or "here is my
  product" every time, on both accounts.
- Put the artifact on screen. Reels where a real thing is visible (a terminal
  session, a cup of noodles in a pool, a highchair) hold; reels that only
  narrate the thing lose half the audience by second three.
- Name a micro-detail and make them watch for it ("there is a hand under it
  the whole time, and at 2.5s it takes over"). Directed attention is the
  single best predictor of a low skip rate in this refresh.
- Open on something the viewer has already met. A universally-recognized
  object or moment in frame one produces the lowest skip rates recorded.
- Never make the account itself the subject. Milestone/meta posts ("this is
  post 51", "here's how to use the app") hold the two worst skip rates in
  this refresh, 69% and 63%, on two unrelated accounts.
- A numbered takeaway list earns saves only when the reel already delivered
  something worth keeping. Bolted onto a reel that gave nothing, the list
  converts nobody.

What works / what dies — @imabgold
- Confession first, credential second. The number ($35M, CFO) only lands as the
  thing he walked away from.
- Machine-as-employee framing is still the strongest recurring shape: "I have a
  CTO, we have never spoken" took 3279 views at a 32% skip, the account's best.
- Real terminal footage on screen is the proof. Timestamps and file dates are
  not; they get skipped.
- The AI catching its OWN mistake outperforms the AI succeeding (Db5_IVZCy7T,
  2658 views, 4 sv / 2 sh). Fallibility is more forwardable than competence.
- Self-referential posts die hardest. Follower counts, post numbers and tooling
  features are inside-the-desk news.
