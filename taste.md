## universal
- **A question the viewer wants to answer out-loud beats a statement.** The four widest
  reels this window are all a stranger's question with one blank in it. The reel that
  merely *describes* the same idea sits 100x lower. Leave the blank; do not close it.
- **The blank must be filled by ONE ownable, picturable OBJECT.** Sharpest line in the
  data, and it still holds a day later. Blanks answered by a single object ran
  36k-776k views at 1.9-4.1% shares. Blanks answered by a LIST, a REASON or an
  abstraction topped out at 4,525 views and 0.29-0.69% shares. Same form, same craft,
  100x apart.
- **Shares, not likes, are the ceiling metric.** The top reel carries 31,458 shares
  against 27,927 likes: more than one share per like, 17 days out. A cut engineered
  to be *sent to someone* outperforms a cut engineered to be admired.
- **Share RATE reads the ceiling early, but give a weak reel 48h before calling it dead.**
  Everything that went wide sits at 1.9-4.1%; everything ordinary sits under 0.7%, and the
  gap is visible within hours. The gap is the signal — the raw view count at 24h is not:
  one reel read 703 views yesterday and 3,107 today, a 4.4x late climb that changed its
  rank but not its band.
- **A low skip rate is a RETENTION signal, not a reach signal — never optimise for it.**
  The three lowest-skip reels this window (27.8%, 29.5%, 33.5%) finished at 2,908-4,525
  views; the four widest sat at 37-44% skip. Holding the people who stayed and being
  handed to new people are different machines.
- **Watch time is the same finding from the other side.** The longest-held reel (15.4s
  average) did 2,908 views; the widest did 775,916 at 7.8s, and the window's #4 by reach
  is the SHORTEST-held reel on the whole account (5.7s). Length holds an audience you
  already have. A premise with a blank is what buys a new one.
- **Payoff last, and only once.** Every high-share reel lands its object in the final
  beat. Every low one answers itself in the first third, then keeps talking.
- **A hook is spent the moment it ships.** Re-running a premise that already worked
  returns a fraction: the same superlative aired twice dropped from 48,732 to 2,487.
