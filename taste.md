## universal
- **A question the viewer wants to answer out-loud beats a statement.** The four biggest
  reels this window are all a stranger's question with one blank in it. The reel that
  merely *describes* the same idea sits 100x lower. Leave the blank; do not close it.
- **Shares, not likes, are the ceiling metric.** The top reel carries 30,146 shares against
  26,534 likes: more than one share per like, 15 days out. A cut engineered to be *sent to
  someone* outperforms a cut engineered to be admired.
- **Share RATE reads the ceiling early, and it keeps holding.** Everything that went wide
  sits at 1.9-4.1% shares per view; everything that floored sits under 0.8%, and the gap
  is visible inside the first day. One ratio, six hours, before views say anything.
- **A low skip rate is a RETENTION signal, not a reach signal — do not optimise for it.**
  Measured this window: the three lowest-skip reels (27.7%, 28.5%, 32.2%) all finished
  under 4,200 views, while the four widest sat at 34-44% skip. Holding the people who
  stayed and being handed to new people are different machines.
- **Watch time and reach are separate machines, same finding from the other side.** The
  longest-held reel (15.5s average) did 2,873 views; the widest did 739,733 at 7.8s.
  Length holds an audience you already have. A premise with a blank is what buys a new one.
- **Payoff last, and only once.** Every high-share reel lands its object in the final
  beat. Every low one answers itself in the first third, then keeps talking.
- **The object that fills the blank has to be OWNABLE and picturable.** Concrete objects
  travel; "a moment", "a feeling", "when it gets real" floored at 620-876 views.
- **A hook is spent the moment it ships.** Re-running a premise that already worked
  returns a fraction: the same superlative aired twice dropped from 48,732 to 2,487.
