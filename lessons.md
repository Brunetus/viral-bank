## DZtdw-PTsHc — @seangstewart: "I cracked talking-head videos (new viral method)" — the STAR method
- source: @seangstewart | https://www.instagram.com/p/DZtdw-PTsHc/
- mechanism: talking-head retention structure — a 5-part opening/format template ("the STAR method"), studied across ~250 videos, that the creator claims every big creator uses
- the-lesson: structure a talking-head reel as **S-T-A-R-R**:
  - **S — Shocking:** open by saying something shocking straight to camera.
  - **T — Text hook:** put an on-screen text hook that COMPLEMENTS (not repeats) the shocking spoken statement.
  - **A — Achievement:** immediately name an achievement/credential that backs up the claim (earns authority to keep watching).
  - **R — Roadmap:** give a visual, step-by-step path showing the viewer how to get from point A to point B.
  - **R — Recipe:** give that roadmap a UNIQUE NAME nobody's heard of — the novel name is a curiosity hook that holds viewers to the very end.
  (Meta: the reel itself uses all five, and reuses the same structure as the creator's "yapping" video — proof the template is repeatable.)
- maps-to: strongly CONFIRMS and sharpens our craft canon. "S + T" = our ARCHITECT-CORE hook law (broad, first-second, on-screen text distinct from the spoken line) and taste.md's "win the first second or nothing downstream matters." "Recipe" (a uniquely named method) is the mechanism behind why our own named formats retain — "the Mbappé Special", "girl grip", "the highchair scan" — the name itself is the curiosity gap; playbook.md's named formats work for this reason. CAVEAT vs taste.md: "A — Achievement" is a credential flex; for imabgold the proven achievement-flex is the identity ("I can't code and built this anyway"), and announcement-shaped/credential openers otherwise underperform — so land A as a relatable identity claim, not a resume line.
- status: TRAINING-ONLY
- file: clips/DZtdw-PTsHc.mp4 (49s)

## DWP7EvrhH7- — @notchais2: "How to get your first million views" — retention-gamified long text + comment-bait
- source: @notchais2 | https://www.instagram.com/p/DWP7EvrhH7-/
- mechanism: two stacked levers on a dead-simple template — (1) gamified retention via unreadable-in-one-loop text, (2) comment-war engagement via controversy. Delivered as a literal recipe
- the-lesson: the exact recipe he gives:
  1. Record a ~3.5s clip of you walking.
  2. In the edit, slow it to 0.5 speed so the clip runs ~7 seconds.
  3. Lay a TRENDING audio over it and MUTE the original audio.
  4. Put a DECENTLY LONG on-screen text stating a controversial opinion in your niche.
  5. Make the text long enough that the average viewer CAN'T finish reading it in 7 seconds — this gamifies retention (they stay/loop to finish reading), which is the whole point.
  6. Make it controversial so viewers who AGREE and who DISAGREE both comment and then argue amongst themselves — comment volume/dwell pushes the video.
- maps-to: CONFIRMS our strongest home-grown insight. Lever (1) is exactly [[ig-reel-rewatch-and-meta-first]] / our "whole joke on one static screen + deliberately short = forced rewatch" rule, generalized: text the viewer can't finish in one loop IS the retention mechanic (don't "fix" the shortness — it's intentional). Lever (2) is comment-bait, the saves/shares+comments play behind playbook.md #13 (algorithm-plea) and #21 (Haaland "nice"). Step 3 restates our audio law (never bake trending audio; add it in-app, mute the original — see audio.md / ARCHITECT-CORE §4). STRONG CAVEAT vs our brands: manufactured controversy is off-brand and risky for @bambi.map (family-safe) and for imabgold's calm founder voice — taste.md shows earnest/story beats win there and CTA/bait reels die. Take the MECHANISM (a text too long to finish in the loop; a genuinely debatable take that invites replies) but NOT the literal "pick a fight" framing. The walking-b-roll template is disposable; the two levers are the lesson.
- status: TRAINING-ONLY
- file: clips/DWP7EvrhH7-.mp4 (44s)
